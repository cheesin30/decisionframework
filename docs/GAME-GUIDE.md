# The Long Game — How to Use It

A plain-English guide to playing and running "The Long Game." No technical
knowledge needed. If you can open a web link, you can run this.

---

## 1 · What it is

"The Long Game" is a short, browser-based investing game. A player is
given a portfolio and lives through six real market moments — a crash, a
panic, a mania — without being told the dates. At each moment they choose to
**Hold, Sell, or Buy**. At the end, the game reveals that every moment was
real, and shows how simply staying invested usually won.

It comes in three editions — pick one for your situation:

| Edition | Best for | Fund content |
|---|---|---|
| **Live Room** | A room of people at an event, each on their phone, with results on a big screen | None (product-agnostic) |
| **Learning** | One person playing solo, on their own device | None (product-agnostic) |
| **Sales** | One person solo, when you want to show a representative balanced fund | Includes the fund panel + chart line |

All three teach the same lesson and play almost identically. The Live Room
adds phones, a leaderboard, and performance checks; the Sales edition adds
fund information at the end.

> **Note:** these are educational demonstrations. The market data is real but
> illustrative, and the games are not investment advice.

## 2 · Playing a solo game (Learning or Sales)

**To start:** open the game link on any device (phone, laptop, tablet).

1. **Choose your amount.** The welcome screen asks how much you have invested.
   Type any amount — it defaults to $50,000. Your money sits in a **Balanced
   fund** (60% equities, 40% fixed income); there's no fund to choose.
2. **Do the practice round.** One warm-up moment that doesn't count, so you
   learn the buttons.
3. **Play the six moments.** For each one you'll read a short, tense story and
   choose:
   - **Hold** — stay fully invested.
   - **Sell 25%** — move a quarter of your money to cash (safety).
   - **Buy 25%** — move a quarter back in (only available after you've sold).
4. **See what each choice would have done.** Right after every choice, the
   game shows how Hold, Sell and Buy each would have turned out that round —
   but never tells you *when* in history you are.
5. **The windfall (moment 3).** Partway through, an unexpected bonus lands —
   half your starting amount is added and invested automatically. It arrives
   right as a scary moment hits, testing whether you add or pull back.
6. **The reveal.** At the end, the game names every moment you lived through
   (COVID crash, the 2022 bear market, and so on) and charts your choices
   against simply staying invested.
7. **Explore other crises.** On the reveal, tap **"See it in another crisis"**
   to replay the same lesson through 1973, 1987, 2000, 2008 and more — the
   shape barely changes. (These historical examples always use a fixed
   $50,000, separate from your game.)
8. **Play again** to try different choices or a different starting amount.

That's the whole game — about four minutes.

## 3 · Running a Live Room event

This is the group version: a big screen at the front, everyone plays on their
own phone, and a live leaderboard drives the room.

### 3.1 What you need

- A **screen at the front** (projector or TV) with a web browser.
- Each participant's **phone** (they'll scan a QR code to join).
- The game open on the big screen from the **hosted web link** (this matters —
  see §5).

### 3.2 Setting up (2 minutes)

1. On the big screen, open the game link and choose **"Host a live room."**
2. A **room code** and **QR code** appear.
3. Set the **starting amount for the room** in the box on the lobby screen
   (default $50,000). Everyone plays this same amount, which keeps the
   leaderboard fair.
4. Ask the room to **scan the QR code** (or type the code) and enter a
   nickname. Names appear on the big screen as people join.
5. When everyone's in, press **"Start the six moments."**

### 3.3 Running the game

You (the host) drive the pace from the big screen. For each moment:

1. The moment's story appears on the big screen and on every phone.
2. People choose Hold / Sell / Buy on their phones. A live tally shows how the
   room is voting — **but the outcome stays hidden.**
3. When the room has voted, press **"Reveal what happened."** A dramatic
   number shows what the market actually did that month.
4. Press **"Next moment"** to continue.

**Performance checks (after moments 2 and 4).** The big screen automatically
shows a leaderboard — every player as a named bar, sorted, against a dotted
"invested" line. Each phone shows that player's own rank ("#4 of 12"). This is
the moment the room feels the competition. Press the button to continue.

**The windfall (moment 3).** A bonus is announced on the big screen and every
phone — half the starting amount, added to everyone at once.

**The finale.** After moment 6, the big screen shows the full results: every
player's finish as a bar chart against the "invested" line and the
"buy-and-hold" line. Each phone then shows its own complete reveal, including
the historical dates and the chart.

### 3.4 Host controls at a glance

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Host a live room** | Creates the room + QR code |
| **Start the six moments** | Begins play once people have joined |
| **Reveal what happened** | Shows the market outcome for the current moment |
| **Next moment** | Advances to the next moment (or the performance check) |

Everything else is automatic.

## 4 · The mechanics, explained

- **Starting amount.** Whatever you (or the host) enter. The whole game scales
  to it. In a Live Room it's one amount for everyone, so the leaderboard
  compares like with like.
- **Balanced fund.** All money sits in a 60/40 Balanced fund. There is no risk
  option to pick — one profile for everyone.
- **Hold / Sell / Buy.** Sell moves 25% to cash; Buy moves 25% back in (only
  after selling); Hold stays put. Cash earns a small assumed return.
- **The round-3 windfall.** An unexpected bonus of 50% of the starting amount,
  invested automatically at the balanced mix. It deliberately lands during a
  scary stretch — the interesting question is whether people add to a shaky
  market or retreat.
- **Performance checks (Live only).** Snapshots after moments 2 and 4 showing
  everyone's standing. The dotted line marks the money put in so far (it steps
  up after the windfall), so "ahead of the line" means "your money grew."
- **The reveal.** Real historical months, in real order, shown only at the
  end — so during play you can't guess where you are, and the ending lands
  harder.
- **Exploring other crises.** The reveal lets you replay the lesson through
  earlier decades to show it isn't a one-off.

## 5 · Live Room hosting notes (important)

**Always host the big screen from the hosted web link,** not a downloaded
copy of the file. A downloaded file has no web address for phones to reach,
and may be an older version. Opening from the hosted link guarantees phones
can join and everyone's on the same version.

**Networks and the QR code:**

- Phones on **any network (including cellular)** can join when you host from
  the hosted web link — the room lives online, so the QR sends phones there.
- If you ever host from a company laptop's own server instead, phones must be
  on the **same network**, and you must open the host screen using the
  laptop's network address (not "localhost"). The on-screen notes and the
  `live-server` guide explain this.
- If a phone can't join, the game shows a clear message with the likely cause
  (usually a corporate network blocking the connection) rather than hanging.

**A quick pre-event test:** open the host screen, scan with one phone, join,
and confirm the name appears on the big screen. Do this before the audience
arrives.

## 6 · What the game is designed to teach

Keep these in mind when you debrief the room:

- **Time in the market beats timing it.** Staying invested through the scary
  moments almost always beat jumping in and out.
- **The best days hide next to the worst.** Sitting out just a handful of the
  best months badly hurt returns — and those best months tend to arrive right
  when it feels most hopeless.
- **The feeling never changes.** Whether it's 1973, 2008 or 2020, the panic
  feels the same in the moment — and holding on was the hard, right call.
- **It's not about being fearless.** It's about having a plan and a long
  horizon, which is exactly what a diversified, professionally managed fund is
  built to support.
