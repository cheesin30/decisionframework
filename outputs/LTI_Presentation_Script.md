# LTI Learning Experience — Presenter Script

**Deck:** `LTI_Learning_Experience_Capital_Learning.pptx` (8 slides)
**Audience:** Capital Learning team · **Runtime:** ~10–12 minutes + live demo + Q&A
**Tone:** conversational, confident, business-impact lens. The slides are visual anchors — you carry the story.

---

## Slide 1 — Cover · "From a workshop to a journey"

*(~45 seconds. Let the slide sit while people settle. Speak plainly.)*

> Hi everyone — thanks for making the time.
>
> I want to walk you through a piece of work that started with a simple observation about our Long-Term Investing workshop: the workshop itself is strong. Really strong. But it's a moment. Advisers walk in, they have a great two hours, and they walk out.
>
> What I've been building is everything **around** that moment — so the workshop stops being an event and becomes a journey. I'll show you what I built, why it's shaped the way it is, and then I'll actually run the whole thing live so you can see it work.

**Transition:** "Let me start with what was missing."

---

## Slide 2 — The Gap · "The workshop began and ended in the room."

*(~1 minute. Point to the empty boxes — let the visual do the arguing.)*

> This was the starting point. In the middle: the LTI workshop — good content, well delivered, no complaints.
>
> But look at either side. **Before** the workshop: nothing. An adviser's first contact with the material is the moment they sit down. **After** the workshop: also nothing. No reinforcement, no follow-up, no way to know whether anything stuck.
>
> And there was a third gap you can't see on the slide: no template. Every consultant who runs this workshop starts from scratch on anything beyond the deck itself.
>
> So the learning lived and died inside one room, on one day.

**Transition:** "That gap was also the opportunity."

---

## Slide 3 — Project Brief · "Freedom to design it end-to-end."

*(~45 seconds. Sweep left to right along the chevron.)*

> Because nothing existed before or after the workshop, there was nothing to inherit — which meant freedom to design the whole journey end-to-end: pre-workshop, in-workshop, post-workshop, as **one experience, not three assets**.
>
> I want to be clear this wasn't built in a vacuum — the team's guidance shaped the direction throughout, and conversations with our consultants in Singapore and the international team fed directly into how the materials are framed.
>
> The design principle was simple: everything connects. What advisers do before the session sets up what happens in the room, and what happens in the room feeds what they receive afterwards.

**Transition:** "So here's what that actually looks like."

---

## Slide 4 — What I Built · "Built the journey around the workshop."

*(~2 minutes. This is the first substance slide — go card by card, left to right.)*

> Three layers, wrapped around the existing workshop.
>
> **First — pre-workshop.** A short survey advisers complete right before the session starts. Two minutes, tap-not-type. It gives them a reason to start thinking about the material before we say a word — and it gives us a read on the room.
>
> **Second — the workshop materials. This is the core of it.** Four pieces, all designed to be printed and put in an adviser's hands after the session:
> - the **tipsheet** — the whole workshop on one page;
> - **steps for success** — the follow-through sequence, so "what do I do Monday morning" has an answer;
> - **advisor talking points** — the language for their own client conversations;
> - and a **client-facing deck** — material they can use directly with clients, verbatim.
>
> The test for every one of these was: *can an adviser use this in a real client conversation without us in the room?*
>
> **Third — post-workshop.** A follow-up survey, plus the piece I'm most excited about: the **engagement calendar tool**, which delivers materials at timed touchpoints after the session. The learning doesn't stop when the room empties — it keeps arriving.

**Transition:** "Each of these exists for a specific reason — let me show you the logic."

---

## Slide 5 — Solutions Overview · "Each material supports a specific moment."

*(~1.5 minutes. This is the 'it was thought through' slide. Emphasise purpose, not features.)*

> Same materials, but now look at the **why** column for each moment.
>
> The pre-workshop survey exists to **understand the starting point** — where each adviser actually is, not where we assume they are.
>
> The advisor toolkit exists to **help advisers continue the conversation** — the workshop gives them conviction; the toolkit gives them the words and the structure to act on it with clients.
>
> And the post-workshop layer exists to **sustain engagement** — because we all know what happens to workshop material after two weeks of silence.
>
> *(Point to the navy band.)* The engagement calendar is the connector. It takes the toolkit materials and sends them out across regular touchpoints — automatically. Nobody has to remember to follow up. The follow-up is built in.

**Transition:** "That's the theory. Now let me show you the real thing."

---

## Slide 6 — Demo Transition · "Now, the flow in action." *(dark slide)*

*(~30 seconds, then switch to the live demo. The dark slide is your cue that the mode is changing.)*

> Rather than describe it any further — let me just show you.
>
> I'll walk through the experience exactly as an adviser would: from the survey before the workshop, to the advisor materials, to the follow-up calendar landing in your inbox.

**LIVE DEMO — run sheet:**
1. **Prepare** — open the pre-workshop survey; complete it on screen (or have a volunteer do it on their phone).
2. **Deliver** — flick through the four toolkit pieces quickly; hold up printed copies if you have them.
3. **Follow up** — open the engagement calendar tool, generate the follow-up, then ask the room:
   > "Now grab your phones — scan this code. In about thirty seconds the LTI follow-up journey will land in your inbox. This is exactly what advisers experience at the end of the workshop."
4. While calendars arrive, narrate what's happening behind the scenes in one sentence. When phones start buzzing, pause — *let the moment land*.

**Transition (back to deck):** "So that's today. One more thing — where this goes next."

---

## Slide 7 — Future Outlook · "Built for now. Ready for what's next."

*(~1 minute. Tone matters here: NOW is not a stopgap.)*

> What you've just seen is ready to use **now** — a practical consultant toolkit. Nothing waiting on a platform, nothing waiting on a build. You could run this at your next workshop tomorrow.
>
> But it was deliberately built with the future in mind. The structure you saw — survey, toolkit, timed follow-ups — is exactly the structure that migrates onto **CI Pro**: hosted in one place, advisers sign up themselves, all the materials live there.
>
> So the work does double duty. It serves advisers today, and it's the working blueprint that makes the CI Pro move straightforward when we're ready — we'd be moving a proven structure, not designing from scratch on a platform.

**Transition:** "That's the journey — from a workshop that ended in the room, to an experience that follows advisers out of it."

---

## Slide 8 — Q&A · "Questions?"

*(Close warmly, then open the floor.)*

> The ask from me is simple: try it with your next LTI workshop. Everything is ready — the survey, the toolkit, the calendar.
>
> Thank you — and I'd love your questions.

**Likely questions — prepared answers:**

| Question | Answer in one breath |
|---|---|
| *How much setup does a consultant need to do?* | Almost none — the materials are ready to print, and the calendar journey is generated in under a minute. |
| *Can this work for other workshops / markets?* | Yes — the structure is workshop-agnostic, and the materials have already been adapted with input from the Singapore and international teams. |
| *What happens to the survey responses?* | They're for the consultant's read on the room — light-touch by design, not a data-capture exercise. |
| *When could CI Pro happen?* | The structure is migration-ready now; timing is a platform conversation, not a rebuild. |
| *Who maintains the materials?* | Content designers own generation; consultants just use them — that split is designed in. |
