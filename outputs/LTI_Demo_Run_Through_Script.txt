Companion to: LTI_Presentation_Script.txt
Starts at:    Slide 6 — "Now, the flow in action."
Runtime:      ~8-10 minutes
Flow:         PREPARE -> DELIVER -> FOLLOW UP
              (same three steps as the slide behind you)

  [ ] Pre-Workshop Diagnostic form open in a browser tab
  [ ] Post-Workshop Survey form open in a second tab
  [ ] The four toolkit PDFs open, in order: Tip Sheet, Talking

------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1 — PREPARE  ·  the pre-workshop survey
------------------------------------------------------------
(~2 minutes. Switch from the deck to the Forms tab.)

SHOW: the Pre-Workshop Diagnostic cover page.

SAY:
  This is the first thing an adviser touches — before the workshop,
  not after. It's a Microsoft Form, four questions, about two
  minutes. Everything is tap, not type.

SCROLL to Question 1 (what do clients say when they hesitate).

SAY:
  Question one: "What do clients often say when they hesitate to
  invest?" — cash feels safer, markets are too risky, waiting for
  better timing, investing feels complicated. Notice these are the
  exact objections the workshop and every material afterwards is
  built around. The adviser is naming their own problem before we
  ever present a solution.

SCROLL to Question 2 (what do you usually do first).

SAY:
  Question two is the quiet diagnostic: "When clients raise these
  concerns, what do you usually do first?" Share market data,
  explain the product, address the concern straight away — or ask
  what is driving the concern before responding. Every option
  sounds reasonable, so the answers are honest. Most people don't
  pick "ask first" — and that gap is exactly what the workshop
  teaches into.

SCROLL to Question 4 (confidence self-rating grid).

SAY:
  And question four is a confidence baseline — three statements,
  rated from Not Confident to Very Confident. Hold that thought,
  because we measure these exact three statements again after the
  workshop. That's how we show the shift.

TRANSITION:
  "So the adviser walks in warmed up, and we walk in knowing the
  room. Then the workshop happens. Here's what they leave with."


------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2 — DELIVER  ·  the advisor toolkit (4 pieces)
------------------------------------------------------------
(~4 minutes. One beat per document. If you printed copies, hold
each one up as you open it.)

--- 2a. CLIENT CONVERSATION TIP SHEET (~1 min) ---

SHOW: the one-pager.

SAY:
  First, the Tip Sheet — the whole method on one page.

  Across the top: the four-box framework — Acknowledge,
  Perspective, Confidence, Opportunity. Then every common
  objection as a row: "investing is unnecessary", "long-term
  investing is risky", "the timing is not right", "it's too
  difficult", "my friends say..." — each with the likely barrier,
  how to respond, and a simple line to use, word for word.

POINT at one "simple line to use" and read it aloud:
  "Cash can feel safe, but over time it may not keep up with what
  you need it to do."

POINT at the bottom strip:
  And it ends with action — confirm the goal, pick one proof
  point, agree a small next step, set follow-up. Start with the
  client's worry and goal, not the product.

--- 2b. ADVISOR TALKING POINTS (~1 min) ---

SHOW: flick through 2-3 pages; stop on "The cost of missing the
market's best days".

SAY:
  Second, the Talking Points — the workshop's strongest charts,
  re-cut for client conversations. Every page has the same three
  boxes: three talking points, one question to ask your client,
  and one advisor next step.

  Take this page: a thousand dollars fully invested became
  $2,959 — miss the best 40 days and you're at $941, below where
  you started. The line to remember: market timing requires two
  correct decisions — when to leave AND when to return.

READ the "Ask your client" line aloud:
  "What would help you stay invested through a period of market
  volatility?"

SAY:
  Chart, question, next step. That's a client conversation, not a
  presentation.

--- 2c. CLIENT BOOKLET (~1 min) ---

SHOW: the cover — "Investing for the long term: Four ideas worth
holding on to".

SAY:
  Third — and this one is client-facing. The adviser hands this
  directly to the client. Four ideas, one per spread:

    1. "Safe" cash carries a quiet risk — even Singapore's modest
       1.74% inflation quietly erodes cash over decades.
    2. Time does the heavy lifting — steady investing since 1994
       grew to roughly $760k, most of it growth, not contributions.
    3. Don't miss the best days — the same $2,959-versus-$941
       story, in client language.
    4. Declines are normal and usually shorter — the average bull
       ran +234% over 73 months; the average bear, -33% over 13.

  And it closes with "Turn four ideas into one plan" — three
  questions the client answers WITH the adviser. The booklet ends
  where the adviser's next meeting begins.

--- 2d. STEPS FOR SUCCESS (~1 min) ---

SHOW: the one-pager.

SAY:
  Fourth, Steps for Success — because none of this matters if it's
  forgotten by Friday. This is an eight-week reinforcement
  sequence: Day 0, set your cue. Day 3, pick one real client line.
  Week 1, practise Acknowledge. Week 2, add Perspective. Week 3,
  move to Opportunity. Week 4, log one win. Week 6, run the full
  four-box flow in a real conversation. Week 8, choose the one
  habit you keep.

  One small action per touchpoint, three minutes each. And notice
  the note at the top: "import the calendar file — when a reminder
  appears, spend 3 minutes on the matching action."

TRANSITION:
  "Which raises the obvious question — who remembers to send all
  of this at the right time? Nobody. So nobody has to."


------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3 — FOLLOW UP  ·  the engagement calendar (+ the scan)
------------------------------------------------------------
(~3 minutes. Switch to the Engagement Calendar tool. This is the
peak of the demo — don't rush the scan moment.)

SHOW: the tool's touchpoint view.

SAY:
  This is the Engagement Calendar. The follow-up journey is
  already built — three touchpoints:

    DAY 0   — Prepare, then capture one real client concern
    WEEK 1  — Build one full response in your next conversation
    MONTH 1 — Keep one habit and reinforce the core message

  Each touchpoint carries its materials from a resource library —
  the same four pieces you just saw. A consultant can adjust the
  sequence, or just use it as-is.

CLICK Export calendar (or show the export button).

SAY:
  One click exports the whole journey as a calendar file. The
  reminders land in the adviser's own calendar, with the materials
  attached. Nothing to remember, nothing to re-send.

NOW the scan moment. Put the QR on screen.

SAY:
  And here's how an adviser gets it at the end of a workshop.
  Grab your phones — scan this code. It opens a short form,
  name and email only. Submit it, and in about thirty seconds
  the full LTI follow-up journey lands in your inbox.

WHILE PHONES ARE OUT (one sentence, no more):
  Behind the scenes, the form triggers an automated flow that
  fetches the right calendar file and emails it — fully inside
  our Microsoft environment, nothing hosted externally.

WHEN PHONES START BUZZING: stop talking. Let the room react.
Then:
  That — is the whole experience. What you just did is exactly
  what advisers do on the closing slide of the workshop.

CLOSE THE LOOP (optional, 20 seconds — flick to the Post-Workshop
Survey tab):
  And before they leave the room, the post-workshop survey re-asks
  the same three confidence questions from the diagnostic — plus
  which objection they now feel most prepared for, and where they
  still want practice. Baseline before, shift after. That's how we
  know it works — and it tells us what the next session should
  focus on.

TRANSITION (back to the deck, Slide 7):
  "So that's today, end to end: survey, toolkit, automated
  follow-up. One more thing — where this goes next."

IF SOMETHING FAILS — FALLBACKS
------------------------------------------------------------

  QR won't scan on the room screen
    -> have the short link ready to read out, or show the email
       arriving on YOUR phone via the room screen.

  Email takes longer than 30 seconds
    -> keep going; circle back when the first phone buzzes:
       "there it is."

  No internet / Forms down
    -> printed toolkit pieces carry Part 2; describe Parts 1 and 3
       over static screenshots (keep a screenshot of each form's
       cover and the calendar tool in the deck's appendix).
